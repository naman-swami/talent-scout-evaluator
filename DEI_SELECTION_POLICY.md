# EEOC Selection Guidelines & Adverse Impact Auditing Policy

## 1. Statutory Basis & Federal Standards
Talent Scout Evaluator operationalizes non-discriminatory hiring analytics under:
- **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq.)**
- **Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (29 CFR Part 1607)**
- **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) Technical Assistance Guidelines**

---

## 2. The EEOC Four-Fifths (80%) Rule Formulation
A selection rate for any race, sex, or ethnic group which is less than four-fifths ($4/5$) or $80\%$ of the rate for the group with the highest selection rate will generally be regarded by federal enforcement agencies as evidence of adverse impact:

$$\text{Adverse Impact Ratio (AIR)} = \frac{SR_{\text{protected}}}{SR_{\text{benchmark}}} \ge 0.80$$

Where:
- $SR_{\text{protected}} = \frac{\text{Hired Protected Candidates}}{\text{Total Protected Applicants}}$
- $SR_{\text{benchmark}} = \frac{\text{Hired Benchmark Candidates}}{\text{Total Benchmark Applicants}}$

### Action Required upon Adverse Impact Finding:
If $\text{AIR} < 0.80$, the hiring process triggers mandatory administrative audit:
1. The selection rubric must be reviewed for artificial barriers that are not job-related.
2. Formal validation study must be conducted under 29 CFR § 1607.5 (Criterion-Related, Content, or Construct Validity).

---

## 3. Structured Behavioral Competency Rubrics (BARS)
To prevent subjective interviewer bias, evaluations are restricted to predefined Behavioral Anchor Rating Scales (BARS):
- **Technical Problem Solving (35% weight)**: Tested against reproducible algorithmic fixtures.
- **System Architecture (30% weight)**: Evaluated for fault tolerance and scalability.
- **Engineering Communication (20% weight)**: Assessed on structured PR feedback and clarity.
- **Production Rigor (15% weight)**: Tested on testing completeness and CI/CD hygiene.
